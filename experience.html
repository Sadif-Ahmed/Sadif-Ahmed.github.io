Seeing Through the Forgery Is Not Enough: Regressive Sycophancy under Fabricated Evidence in Medical Vision-Language Models

Under review, ACL Rolling Review (October 2026) arXiv preprint Independent Research

Medical vision-language models (VLMs) often drop a correct answer when a user pushes back with a fabricated document. We study this regressive sycophancy with four open VLMs on 400 VQA-RAD yes/no questions in 19,200 multi-turn conversations. When the models are not told that the documents are synthetic, they abandon 80.1% of 6,488 correct answers. Models often reject the fake prescription in their reasoning and still adopt its answer, so knowing about a forgery does not stop the flip.

AgentTell: Behavioural Side-Channel Leakage in Browser-Use Agents

Under review, ICLR 2027 arXiv preprint Independent Research

A browser-use agent can learn a private fact on one website and reveal it through its actions on another, even when told not to disclose it. We call this behavioural side-channel leakage and measure it with AgentTell, a benchmark of 20 scenarios and 100 tasks. Across 9,760 sessions on six backbone models, agents that had learned the secret revealed it in 61.1% of the 7,630 sessions where they carried it. In 34.5% of leaking sessions, the agent's final response told the user that the secret had not been disclosed.

IssueGuard: Real-Time Secret Leak Prevention Tool for GitHub Issue Reports

✓ FSE 2026, Tool Demo Track arXiv Oct 2025 – Feb 2026 Undergraduate Thesis

IssueGuard is a Chrome extension that warns users about secrets in GitHub and GitLab issue reports as they type. It extracts candidates with regular expressions and classifies them with a fine-tuned CodeBERT model, reaching an F1 score of 92.70%. The average end-to-end latency is 198 ms. In a user study with 50 participants, 40 (80%) rated their confidence in its detection as very high.

Secret Leak Detection in Software Issue Reports using LLMs: A Comprehensive Evaluation

✓ MSR 2026, Technical Track arXiv Jul 2024 – Oct 2025 Undergraduate Thesis

A large-scale study of secrets exposed in GitHub issue reports. Our pipeline extracts candidate secrets with regular expressions and classifies them with large language models (LLMs) to cut false positives. We built a benchmark of 54,148 instances from public GitHub issues, including 5,881 manually verified secrets. Fine-tuned open-source LLMs such as Qwen and LLaMA reach an F1 score of up to 94.49%. On 178 real-world GitHub repositories, the approach reaches an F1 score of 81.6%.

A Survey on Agentic Security: Applications, Threats and Defenses

✓ TMLR (accepted) ICLR 2026 AIWILD Workshop (non-archival) arXiv Aug 2025 – Oct 2025 Independent Research

A survey of more than 260 papers on LLM-based agents in cybersecurity. It organizes the field around three pillars: applications, threats and defenses. It maps which defenses cover which attack classes. It concludes that agentic systems are structurally fragile by default, so their defenses must span the full agent lifecycle.

Secret Breach Detection in Source Code with Large Language Models

✓ ESEM 2025, Technical Track arXiv Oct 2024 – Jul 2025 Undergraduate Thesis

We detect secrets in source code by extracting candidates with regular expressions and classifying them with large language models. On a balanced 3,000-sample subset of SecretBench, a LLaMA-3.1-8B model fine-tuned with QLoRA reaches an F1 score of 98.52%. This beats GPT-4o with few-shot prompting (93.92%) and regex-only baselines. Fine-tuned open-source models are a practical alternative to commercial APIs and can be deployed locally.

arXiv preprint
